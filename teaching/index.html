Teaching & Mentoring

I teach machine learning and brain–machine interfaces alongside the signal processing and embedded systems that support them. My courses take students from mathematical models to working systems. In signals courses, students predict what a transform will reveal, derive the result, and test it on recorded data. In embedded labs, they explain how their code responds to new sensor data and interrupts.

Open teaching materials

Hands-on Brain–Computer Interfaces

This workshop uses runnable MNE-Python notebooks to teach EEG signal analysis. Students inspect recordings, identify event-related potentials and sensorimotor rhythms, and build decoders for P300, motor imagery, and steady-state visual evoked potential tasks. Workshop notebooks on GitHub

Open-Weight LLMs & Agentic AI

I developed this four-day ROBT 407 workshop for students comfortable with Python. They run Qwen models locally, compare model sizes and quantization, validate structured outputs, and build a retrieval system that cites its sources. They implement an agent loop, test when it fails, and bring the pieces together in a small assistant they can evaluate against a test set. The workshop includes local and Colab notebooks. Workshop repository · Course site

Machine Learning Engineering: From Models to Systems

A practical textbook for readers who can already train models and want to build systems around them. Its four parts cover reproducible experiments, data validation and leakage, model evaluation and serving, deployment and monitoring, and larger systems for ranking, retrieval, and deep learning. Chapters pair written explanations with runnable Colab labs, checked exercises, and worked solutions. Textbook and labs on GitHub

YouTube lectures and tutorials

I share video lessons on signal processing, machine learning, and Python for engineers. Students can use them to revisit a derivation or follow a coding example at their own pace. Berdakh Abibullaev on YouTube

Mentoring

I have supervised three PhD and eight MSc students to completion and advised undergraduate capstone projects. I ask students to define a testable question, build a baseline, and document how they evaluated it before moving to more complex methods. One example is our student-first-authored work on compact EEG transformers, which pairs an evaluation on held-out participants with a public implementation. Paper · Code
